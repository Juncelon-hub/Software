• Automotive headlight systems
• ADB or glare-free high beam
• Sequential turn/animated daytime running lights
3 Description
The TPS92662A-Q1 LED matrix manager device
enables fully dynamic adaptive lighting solutions by
providing individual pixel-level LED control. The
device includes four sub-strings of three series-
connected integrated switches for bypassing
individual LEDs. The individual sub-strings allow the
device to accept either single or multiple current
sources.
The TPS92662A-Q1 features a programmable Pierce
crystal oscillator driver. Optimal performance is
achieved by selecting the driver strength based on
the quartz crystal or ceramic resonator manufacturers
recommendations. The device also incorporates a
selectable drive strength clock buffer. The rise and
fall times and the EMI generated by clock signal is
controlled by varying the drive strength of the clock
buffer. When necessary, the clock buffer can be
disabled to eliminate the noise generated by the clock
signal and provide the lowest EMI performance.
The TPS92662A-Q1 supports the multi-drop universal
asynchronous receiver transmitter (UART) serial
interface and is compatible with TPS92662-Q1 and
TPS92663-Q1 devices. The I 2C communication
interface can be used to read from and write to an
external EEPROM that can store system calibration
data.
An on-board 8-bit ADC with two multiplexed inputs
can be used for system temperature compensation
and used to measure a binning value which allows for
LED binning and coding.

5 Description (continued)
The internal charge pump rail supplies the gate drive voltage for the LED bypass switches. The low on-resistance
(RDS(on)) of the bypass switch minimizes conduction loss and power dissipation.
The TPS92662A-Q1 and TPS92662-Q1 both incorporate identical register settings for programming phase shift
and pulse width of each individual LED in the string and for reporting LED open and short circuit faults.

8 Detailed Description
8.1 Overview
The TPS92662A-Q1 lighting matrix manager device, in conjunction with buck switching current regulators,
enables a fully dynamic matrix beam solution where each LED can be individually controlled. This type of control
of an LED array is ideally suited for dynamic headlight applications where adaptive beam forming requires pixel-
level control of the array.
The TPS92662A-Q1 device configures four sub-strings of three-series-connected integrated switches
(MOSFETs) for bypassing individual LEDs. The series-connected internal switches can float up to 62 V above
ground potential, as long as the absolute maximum voltage across the drain or source of each switch is not
violated. Refer to the Absolute Maximum Ratings . Each switch connects in parallel to one (or multiple series-
connected) LED or LEDs, thereby creating individual shunt paths across each LED. For high-power LED devices,
the TPS92662A-Q1 device can be programmed to operate in parallel configuration, such that multiple of the
internal switches are configured in parallel mode, shunting one single LED. Furthermore, LED strings with fewer
LEDs can be used. Nevertheless, the unused channels must be physically shorted externally to reduce
unnecessary internal power consumption.
Each switch has an individual driver, programmable overvoltage protection circuit, and diagnostics circuit
referenced to the source of that switch. This configuration allows for fully dynamic operation with the switches
above it and below it. The device monitors overvoltage conditions on each switch and automatically protects
them in the event of an open LED connection. The device detects open LED conditions as well as shorted LED
conditions and reports them through the fault reporting network.
All twelve internal bypass switches can be individually pulse width modulated (PWM) at a programmed
frequency, phase-shift, and duty cycle. This PWM dimming topology provides inherent phase shifting capability.
In addition, the switch transitions during PWM dimming have a programmable slew rate limit to mitigate any EMI
concerns due to the di/dt and dv/dt of the switching action.
The TPS92662A-Q1 device also provides multi-drop UART communications capability between a host
microcontroller unit (MCU) and up to 31 slave TPS92662A-Q1 devices. The UART receives data corresponding
to the desired PWM information for all 12 internal switches. In addition, it can send back fault and other
diagnostic data to the host MCU. Hardware connections on the three address pins allow addressing of up to 31
devices. An application can use the TPS92662A-Q1 device and the TPS92663-Q1 device on the same bus.
The TPS92662A-Q1 device includes an on-board 8-bit ADC with two multiplexed general purpose inputs, which
can be used for system temperature compensation, binning and coding, and so forth. Furthermore, one of the
address pins of the device is connected to the input of the ADC to extend the addressability of the TPS92662A-
Q1 from 8 to 31 devices.
The internal regulator accepts battery voltage on input pin VIN (as high as 60 V) and generates a 5.0-V regulated
output for the internal digital and analog circuitry. The internal regulator can be bypassed by shorting VIN to VDD
if an external regulator is being used.
The combination of features in the TPS92662A-Q1 device provides the ideal interface for individual control of
high current LED arrays. As shown in the Application Information section, the electronic control unit (ECU),
usually attached to the outside of the headlight, contains the master MCU, a boost pre-regulator stage that takes
the variable battery voltage and steps it up to a stable DC voltage rail. The dynamic headlight application usually
includes multiple channels of buck current regulators to provide a stable current for each series string of high
brightness LEDs. For the best performance, the TPS92662A-Q1 device resides on the LED load board where it
is as close as possible to the LEDs to which it directly connects. This location has two major benefits:
• The close proximity minimizes distributed inductance and parasitic capacitance associated with the cable
connection between ECU board and LED load board. When PWM dimming with a parallel shunt FET is
performed, placing the switch close to the LED prevents large ringing during each transition.
• The close proximity offers better thermal connection.
Ultimately, the TPS92662A-Q1 device enables an optimal partition for a flexible, high-performing dynamic
headlight system.

8.3 Feature Description
8.3.1 Controlling the Internal LED Bypass Switches
The TPS92662A-Q1 device (LED Matrix Manager) consists of four independent three-series-connected bypass
switches between terminals LED12 and LED10K, LED9 and LED7K, LED6 and LED4K, and LED3 and LED1K
as shown in the Functional Block Diagram . Each bypass switch, when driven to an off state, allows the string
current to flow through the corresponding parallel-connected LED, turning the LED on. Conversely, driving the
bypass switch to an on state shunts the current through the bypass switch and turns the LED off.
8.3.2 Internal Switch Resistance
Each single switch (connected between LEDx and LEDx–1) has a measurable typical R DS(on) value of 200 m Ω.
This measurement includes the actual on-resistance (R DS(on)) of the switch and the resistance of the two
internally-connected bond wires. When multiple series switches are on, the effective resistance is not simply the
number of channels multiplied by 200 m Ω because there are not two conducting bond wires for every series
connected switch. The on-resistance of the 3×series-connected switches (R DS(on)-3LEDs) is specified in the
Electrical Characteristics. This value includes three R DS(on) on-resistances and the resistance of the bond wires at
each end of the series connected switches.
The dominant power loss mechanism in the TPS92662A-Q1 device is the I 2R loss through the switches. Other
power loss sources are typically less than 150 mW. When calculating the power dissipation of the TPS92662A-
Q1 device switches, use Table 1 for the best estimation of the power loss. It is important to note that the power
dissipation is PWM duty cycle dependent. When PWM dimming is applied to the LEDs with duty cycle D, the

8.3.3 PWM Dimming
The TPS92662A-Q1 device provides 10-bit phase-shifted PWM dimming of each individual LED. The LED
phase-shift and pulse-width are separately programmed for each LED via the PHASEx and WIDTHx registers
respectively (where x = 1 to 12). Depending on the value of PSON bit (in SYSCFG register), the TPS92662A-Q1
device implements phase shifting of LED turnon time (PSON = 1) or LED turnoff time (PSON = 0). The pulse-
width of the LEDs can be set by programming the WIDTHx register value. The free-running 10-bit PWM period
counter (TCNT) that increments based on the PWM clock pulse determines the PWM period. The TCNT counts
continuously from 0 to 1023 and wraps back to 0 again. The LED PWM dimming period is 1024 times the
internally divided, programmable PWM clock period.

The PWM clock that drives TCNT is a divided-down version of the CLK input. The divider is programmed by

Upon power up, the PWM clock divider is programmed to a divisor value of 16.

8.3.6 PWM Synchronization
Upon power up, the TCNT counter is reset to 0. The TCNT counter is incremented by the PWM clock. To
synchronize multiple TPS92662A-Q1 devices, two conditions must be met:
• All devices must be clocked by the same clock on the CLK or XTALI pins.
• All devices must be programmed with the same PWMTICK value.
Assuming the above conditions are met, the TPS92662A-Q1 devices can be synchronized by either Hardware
Synchronization or Software Synchronization.
8.3.6.1 Hardware Synchronization
As shown in Figure 12 , the TPS92662A-Q1 device has a synchronization input and output pin (SYNC) and two
synchronization register bits (SYNCOEN and SYNCPEN) in the System Configuration Register (SYSCFG). If
SYNCOEN is set to “1”, the TSP92662 drives the output buffer on the SYNC pin, otherwise, the SYNC pin driver
is tri-stated and operates as an input.
If SYNCOEN is set to “0”, a high pulse on the SYNC pin for at least two CLK periods resets both TCNT and the
PWM clock to 0. SYNC is a feedthrough signal that can be tied to the next TPS92662A-Q1 devices to
synchronize multiple devices with respect to each other.

8.3.6.2 Software Synchronization
The TCNT counter and the PWM clock can both be reset to 0 at any time by issuing a broadcast write
synchronization command (writing a 1 to the SSYNC bit of the SOFTSYNC register). Due to UART bit sampling
variability, the synchronization is achieved within four CLK cycles between TPS92662A-Q1 devices.

(tRISE1/FALL1), medium (t RISE2/FALL2), and slow (t RISE3/FALL3) slew rate (see the Electrical Characteristics for more

8.3.9 LED Fault Detection and Protection
Each individual bypass switch is driven by a floating driver which is powered by the charge pump (see the
Functional Block Diagram ). The floating analog circuitry provides continuous protection and monitoring of LED
open and short events. In the event of an OPEN LED failure, an internal comparator monitors the drain-to-source
voltage of the internal switch. If the voltage exceeds the programmable threshold voltage of V TH-O, the device
overrides the switch programmed WIDTHx value and turns on the switch. This action maintains current flow in
the rest of the LED string in the presence of a faulty or damaged LED, and protects the internal switch from
Electrical Overstress (EOS). The internal latch holds this state until a subsequent LED turnoff event. The
protection circuit also sets the corresponding bit in the Fault Registers . The MCU can poll this register to
determine whether a fault has occurred.
The threshold voltage, V TH-O, can be programmed independently for each LED sub-string (3×series-connected
switches) via a 2-bit value in the OVLMT register. The programmed value provides low (V TH-O1), medium (V TH-O2),
and high (V TH-O3) threshold values. The programmability of the overvoltage thresholds helps applications where
multi-chip LEDs with higher forward voltage drops are used. Furthermore, in applications where significant
ringing occurs during switching events that can result in an undesired overvoltage triggering, higher OVLMT
thresholds can help mitigate the problem.
When the device is configured as described in the Parallel Switch Setting section, digital circuitry of the device
turns on all of the corresponding switches, if one of the parallel switches detects an overvoltage condition. This
allows all of the paralleled switches to share the shunted current instead of only a single switch handling the full
LED current.
Similar to LED open detection, LED short is detected by monitoring the drain-to-source voltage of the internal
switch. If the voltage does not exceed the V TH-S threshold by the end of the PWM cycle, the same fault register
bit is set. Both short and open events are reported by the same register as each event has the equivalent
outcome, which is the LED is shorted.
The synchronized fault inputs are latched into the FAULT registers on the falling edge of the requested LED ON
time. This allows the MCU to determine which LEDs had an open or short fault at the end of the LED ON pulse.
The MCU can clear the fault bits in the FAULT register by writing a 0 to the corresponding bit, provided that the
corresponding fault input is no longer active.

8.3.10 Communications Watchdog Timer
The TPS92662A-Q1 logic has a communications watchdog timer that is operated with the system clock (CLK).
The watchdog timer must be enabled in order to be used. This is accomplished by setting the CMWEN bit to 1 in
the SYSCFG register and programming the communications watchdog timer tap point by writing the desired
value to the CMWTAP register.
The tap point defines the timing of the communications watchdog timer (a 24-bit counter). By default, the tap
point is set to bit 21. More specifically, the device requires 2 21 clock cycles for the communication watchdog timer
to time out. Refer to the CMWTAP register for more information.
If a transaction with a correct CRC is not received for the programmed number of system clock cycles, the
communications watchdog timer times out and the LEDs are pulse width modulated according to the
programmed values in the PHASE and DEFWIDTH registers. The normal PWM duty cycles are resumed based
on the PHASE and WIDTH register settings, only after a valid UART command frame with a correct CRC is
received. The communications watchdog timer is not reset by a communication reset command.
8.3.11 Parallel Switch Setting
The TPS92662A-Q1 can be configured to operate when the switches are connected in parallel. The parallel
switch configuration enables operation of high current LEDs and decreasing power loss and heat. It is important
to note that the physical parallel connection must be performed external to the TPS92662A-Q1. The parallel
configuration of the TPS92662A-Q1 enables simultaneous PWM switching and fault handling of parallel switches.
The TPS92662A-Q1 device includes four sub-strings of S1, S2, S3, and S4 with 3×series-connected switches, as
shown in Figure 18. The combinations of LED strings that can be paralleled are:
• None (each sub-string of bypass switches is independent from the others)
• One pair (S1 and S2 are connected in parallel, S3 and S4 are independent)
• Two pairs (S1 and S2 are connected in parallel, S3 and S4 are connected in parallel)
• All (S1, S2, S3, and S4 are all connected in parallel)
At the end of the power-up sequence, the default parallel setting is none; each LED sub-string is independent.
The controller must program the PARLED register to set the appropriate paralleling configuration. When using
parallel operation, refer to Table 19 for programming WIDTHx values.
Parallel operation also affects the fault management. When sub-strings of LEDs are paralleled, there is a
possibility that one switch senses an overvoltage condition and turns on before the other parallel switches do. If
this situation were not addressed, the switch that clamps the overvoltage condition continued being the only
switch conducting a potentially large current. Therefore, the programming of the PARLED register also configures
the combinations of faults, which causes the gate drivers of all parallel switches to turn on in an overvoltage
condition. There is a small delay due to synchronizing the incoming faults to the internal clock domain, after
which all parallel switches that have an overvoltage condition are immediately turned on to share the LED
currents.

8.3.12 ADC Operation and Settings
The TPS92662A-Q1 device includes an on-board 8-bit ADC with two multiplexed general purpose inputs, which
can be used for system temperature compensation, binning (coding), and so forth. Furthermore, one of the
address pins of the device is connected to the input of the ADC to extend the addressability of the TPS92662A-
Q1 from 8 to 31 devices. The fourth input to the ADC comes from the DSTR pin, which is sampled at start-up to
determine the drive strength of the CLK. Refer to Drive Strength Register – Read-Only for details on DSTR.
The two ADC inputs of ADC1 and ADC2 are periodically sampled in turn every 320 CLK cycles. The two 8-bit
results are stored in ADC1 and ADC2 registers. The reference voltage for the ADC is VDD = 5.0 V (19.5
mV/LSB) nominal.
The third input to the internal ADC is ADDR0. This input is sampled by the ADC only after the TPS92662A-Q1
device receives a broadcast write to the ADCID register. If ADCID is written to a value of 1 or 2, then the ADDR0
input is selected and the ADDR0 input is sampled continuously until there are at least four consecutive ADC
samples with the same value (at a resolution of two bits if ADCID is written to 1, or at a resolution of three bits if
ADCID is written to 2). After the four matching values are obtained, the logic begins sampling the ADC1 and
ADC2 inputs in an alternating fashion. The UART protocol state machine is suspended until the sampling of
ADDR0 is successfully completed.

8.4 Device Functional Modes
The TPS92662A-Q1 device operates in one of several modes as shown in Figure 20 :
Figure 20. TPS92662A-Q1 Functional Mode
8.4.1 Failsafe Mode At Power-Up
The TPS92662A-Q1 device has a failsafe LED operation mode at power up. Upon exiting power-on reset, the
states of the SCL and SDA inputs are checked. If SCL is low and SDA is high, then the duty cycles of all LEDs
are set to 100% by setting the WIDTH registers to 1023. Otherwise, the LED duty cycles are all set to 0% by
setting the WIDTH registers to 0. The microcontroller can then override this initial setting by writing a new value
to the WIDTH register or registers.
8.4.2 Functional Mode
In functional mode, the digital circuitry controls the TPS92662A-Q1 outputs by accepting UART serial commands
and by monitoring and responding to realtime events on the inputs associated with the analog and power
circuitry. This allows PWMing of the LED outputs using the PHASE and WIDTH register settings. See the PWM
Dimming section.
8.4.3 Default PWM (Communications Watchdog Timeout)
This mode allows you to program default pulse width settings for the LEDs, which are used only if the
communication is broken. If the communications watchdog timer is enabled and subsequently times out
(meaning that no successful, error-free UART communications have been received for the number of system
clock cycles programmed via the CMWLMT register), then the LED pulse widths are set to the ones programmed
in the DEFWIDTH registers. The LED phase shift times are set by the programmed values in the PHASE
registers.

The internal logic circuits of the TPS92662A-Q1 operate on the external clock applied to XTALI or CLK pins. The
device also includes an on-chip oscillator for the charge pump circuitry.

8.4.5 UART Interface
The MCU communicates with the TPS92662A-Q1 device using a Universal Asynchronous Receiver and
Transmitter (UART). The pairs of TX and RX pins on the TPS92662A-Q1 device are feedthrough pins, and either
pin can be used to connect the TPS92662A-Q1 device to the network. Feedthrough pins enable the use of metal
core board with single layer routing. The UART communication process uses a command and response protocol
mastered by the MCU to write and read the registers to and from each TPS92662A-Q1 device. The registers are
mapped into an address space on each device. See the TPS92662A-Q1 Registers section for the register list.
8.4.5.1 UART Physical Layer
The TPS92662A-Q1 UART interface uses half-duplex communications (transmit and receive can not overlap). A
tri-state buffer drives the TX pin. This allows multiple TPS92662A-Q1 devices to share a common pair of TX and
RX signals by connecting all TPS92662A-Q1 TX lines together and all TPS92662A-Q1 RX lines together as
shown in Figure 22 . This topology requires two independent wires for UART communication. It is recommended
to place an external pullup resistor on both the RX input return line of the microcontroller unit and on the TX input
to the TPS92662A-Q1 devices. This defines both the TX and RX to the inactive state while the MCU is powering
up.

8.4.5.3 TX Bus Parking
The TPS92662A-Q1 device implements parking for the TX bus. This means that all the devices on the bus are
actively driving TX high when in idle. This feature provides better immunity to Electro-Magnetic Susceptibility
(EMS) in comparison with a floating or tri-stated output.
After a read command or when an acknowledge character after a write command is requested (ACKEN register
bit is set to “1”), the TX buffer outputs of the non-addressed TPS92662A-Q1 devices are tri-stated. This allows
the addressed device to respond. The required timing when a device releases the TX output is dependent on the
configuration of the network. The SEPTR bit in SYSCFG register determines if direct UART connection (SEPTR
= “1”) or the CAN transceiver configuration (SEPTR = “0”) is used.
To prevent bus contention between devices in the CAN transceiver topology, padding is added to the beginning
and end of all write acknowledges and read responses. All non-addressed devices tristate the TX output for eight
clock cycles (equal to one half of the bit time on UART) before the addressed device begins transmitting.
Furthermore, a duration of eight input clock cycles is added to the end of the TX tristate time after the final STOP
bit is received and before it is driven high. The following list summarizes the sequence for TX bus parking.
1. All devices drive the TX bus high.
2. Non-addressed devices tristate the TX output, and only the addressed device continues driving the TX bus
high.
3. After a delay of eight clock cycles, the addressed device begins driving TX low with the first START bit.
4. The non-addressed devices continue tri-stating the TX outputs for eight more clock cycles after the final
STOP bit, and the addressed device continues driving TX high.
5. Finally, all devices resume driving the TX bus high.
This sequence pads the response to account for differences in clock frequencies between devices, as well as
delay in the loopback path of the CAN transceivers.

8.4.5.4 Bus Turnaround Time
When considering back-to-back UART data transfer, there are two cases where additional time is required
between the end of the previous frame STOP bit and the beginning of the next frame START bit. This time is
also known as the minimum required bus turnaround time.
No turnaround time is required in the following cases:
• SEPTR = 1 (direct UART connection) for all back-to-back transfers
• SEPTR = 0 (CAN transceiver configuration) for back-to-back write commands
• SEPTR = 0 and ACKEN=0 between a write command and a read command
Turnaround time is required in the following cases:
• SEPTR = 0 and ACKEN = 1 between a write acknowledge and the next read or write command
• SEPTR = 0 between a read response and the next read or write command frame
In the cases where turnaround time is required, a finite amount of dead time must be inserted between the end
of the STOP bit of the previous frame and the beginning of the START bit of the next frame. There must be at
least 0.5 bit times of space inserted with the TX line of the MCU high following the final STOP bit of the previous
frame before the falling edge of the first START bit of the next frame. This is the minimum delay allowed for the
TPS92662A-Q1 UART to prepare for the next incoming frame. However, this dead time is also dependent upon
the delay of the loopback path through the CAN transceiver and, therefore, can need to be extended, depending
on the timing specifications of the CAN transceiver. Figure 26 illustrates this transaction.

8.4.5.5 UART Communications Reset
The MCU unit can reset the device UART and protocol state machine at any time by holding the RX input low for
a period of at least 12 bit times (16 × 12 CLK periods). This period signifies a break in communications and
causes the TPS92662A-Q1 devices on the network to reset to a known-good state for receiving the next
command frame. The device immediately aborts any response frames in progress.
NOTE
A communications reset does not reset the registers and does not halt normal LED PWM
operation.
8.4.5.6 UART Communications Protocol
The UART communication process uses a command and response protocol mastered by the MCU to write and
read the registers to and from each TPS92662A-Q1 device. This means that the TPS92662A-Q1 device never
initiates traffic onto the network. The protocol maps the registers into an address space on each device. All of the
registers are read-write (R/W). See the TPS92662A-Q1 Registers section for a register list.
The MCU uses the protocol to initiate a communication transaction by sending a command frame. This command
frame addresses either one TPS92662A-Q1 device directly or broadcasts to all TPS92662A-Q1 devices on the
network. This addressing can cause a response frame to be sent back from the slave TPS92662A-Q1 device,
depending on the command. There are three types of command frames:
• Broadcast Write Command: To all TPS92662A-Q1 devices (1, 2, 3, 4, 12, 16, or 32 bytes)
• Single Device Write Command: To a specific TPS92662A-Q1 device (1, 2, 3, 4, 12, 16, or 32 bytes)

8.4.5.7.3 REGADDR
The starting register address is a single byte. The protocol allows writing or reading up to 32 successive register
locations starting from the addressed register to be accomplished with a single command. The starting register
address byte identifies the first TPS92662A-Q1 register being written or read, as described in the TPS92662A-
Q1 Registers section.
The starting register address byte is present in only write and read command transactions, not in read response
transactions. Multi-byte transfers that go beyond address 0xFF automatically wrap back to address 0x00 and
continue.
The following two register addresses are handled differently than the other register addresses.
• I2CFIFODATA: Once a multi-byte write or read reaches the I2CFIFODATA address, the register address-
pointer is no longer incremented, allowing for multiple bytes to be transferred to and from the I 2C data FIFO
using a multi-byte transfer UART frame.
• ADCID: The ADCID register address is also a special location. Any write to this location must be a single-byte
broadcast write. The UART protocol state machine is reset after processing the ADCID broadcast write. Any
multi-byte or single-device write frames that encompass the ADCID register are considered illegal transfers
and are completely discarded, and none of the registers are written. Instead, the protocol state machine
returns immediately to the reset state. Furthermore, no write acknowledge is transmitted following the illegal
write frame.
8.4.5.7.4 DATA Bytes
The frame initialization byte specifies the number of data bytes to be included in the frame.
8.4.5.7.5 CRCL and CRCH Bytes
The CRCL and CRCH bytes (CRC low-byte and CRC high-byte, respectively) are calculated on incoming
command frames using CRC-16-IBM and allow detection of errors within the frame. The CRC error count register
(CERRCNT) is incremented each time a CRC error occurs on an incoming read or write command (see the
TPS92662A-Q1 Registers section for details). The CRC bytes are also calculated by the addressed device
during the read response. The CRC bytes are then appended to the end of the read data, low-byte first. This
allows the master to check the read data coming from the slave device for any transmission errors.
8.4.5.7.6 ACK Byte
Following a successfully received write command, the addressed device sends back an ACK byte (0x7F) if the
ACKEN bit is set in the SYSCFG register.

8.4.6 I 2C Master for External EEPROM
The TPS92662A-Q1 contains a UART-to-I 2C bridge that allows the master MCU to access a local EEPROM via
the UART interface. This feature of the TPS92662A-Q1 allows the wiring harness between the ECU and the LED
load board to have two fewer connections and simplifies the hardware interconnect. The I 2C master supports 7-
bit addressing mode at SCL frequencies of 400 kHz or less. The I 2C master logic is not fully featured and does
not support multi-master or clock stretching since it is primarily intended to communicate with an EEPROM
device that stores lighting module configuration data.
In order for the I 2C master to be used, external pullups must be tied from SDA and SCL to the VDD rail. The
I2CTICK register must be programmed to attain the desired I 2C bit-rate, and the I2CEN bit must be set to ‘1’ in
the SYSCFG register.
Upon issuing a read or write command (by writing the START bit to 1 in the I2CCTRL register), the I 2C master
first checks the state of the bus by making sure that SDA is high. If SDA is low, the EEPROM can be stuck in an
odd state. The I 2C master issues up to 15 clock pulses on SCL to clear out the condition, checking the state of
SDA at each clock. If SDA is still low after 15 clocks, then the SDALOW bit is set in the I2CSTAT register and
the transaction is aborted. If SDA has been released during the attempt to clear the bus, then the requested
transaction begins with a START condition.
8.4.6.1 I 2C Data Format with 7-Bit Address
Data transfers follow the format shown in Figure 27 . After the START condition, a slave address is transmitted.
This address is seven bits long followed by an eighth bit, which is a data direction bit ('R/W'). If the R/W bit is
zero, it indicates a transmit operation (send). If it is one, it indicates a request for data (receive). A data transfer
is always terminated by a STOP condition generated by the master. However, a master can initiate
communications with another device on the bus by generating a repeated START condition and addressing
another slave without first generating a STOP condition. Various combinations of receive and transmit formats
are then possible within a single transfer.

To perform an I 2C EEPROM read, the MCU must program the slave address (I2CSLA), the byte address
(I2CADR), and the read length (I2CRLEN). Next, the MCU must issue the read command by writing the START
and RD bits of the I2CCTRL register to 1. Once the read command is issued, there are two cases that must be
handled differently:
1. Read length greater than or equal to the receive FIFO depth
2. Read length less than the receive FIFO depth
These are described in more detail in the following sections. Note that the receive FIFO depth is 16 bytes.
8.4.6.4.1 Read Length Greater Than or Equal to Receive FIFO Depth
In case the read length is greater than or equal to the receive FIFO depth of 16, the I 2C master reads enough
data bytes to fill the receive FIFO. When the receive FIFO is full, the RFF bit is set to 1 in the I2CSTAT register
and the I 2C master pauses and waits for the receive FIFO to become less than full before it continues to read
from the EEPROM. In this case, the MCU waits until the RFF bit is 1, and then reads all of the receive FIFO data
from the I2CDAT register. The MCU can repeat this sequence of checking for the RFF bit and reading the entire
receive FIFO depth until the remainder of data to be read from the EEPROM is less than the receive FIFO depth.
At this point, the MCU can use the technique described in the next section to read the remaining data from the
FIFO.
8.4.6.4.2 Read Length Less Than Receive FIFO Depth
In case the read length is less than the receive FIFO depth (less than 16), the MCU can simply allow all of the
EEPROM reads to take place and wait for the BUSY bit to be cleared, indicating that the entire read sequence is
finished. After the BUSY bit is cleared, the SLANACK, ADRNACK, SDALOW, and BUSYERR bits must be
checked. If any of these bits are 1, the read command can be re-tried by writing the RST bit to 1 first to reset the
EEPROM read state machine and flush the receive FIFO, then writing the START and RD bits to 1 again. If none
of these ERROR bits are 1, then the MCU can read the data using an optimized set of UART reads, depending
on how many bytes of data are left to read. The data can be read from the I2CDAT register using combinations
of 1, 2, 3, 4, or 12 byte UART protocol reads.

8.5.1 Phase and Width Registers: LED Turnon and Turnoff Control
There are 10-bit registers to control the phase shift and pulse width of the LEDs with respect to the PWM counter
(TCNT). These registers are the PHASE and WIDTH registers, respectively. These registers can be written or
read using several register maps. These different maps all communicate with a common set of registers
internally. The PHASE and WIDTH values are interpreted as follows:
• PHASE[9:0] defines the count value within the 10-bit TCNT period when a start pulse is sent to a given PWM
generator.
• WIDTH[9:0] defines the count value within the 10-bit PWM generator when the LED pulse ends.
0% Duty Cycle: If WIDTH = 0 for a given LED, that LED turns off and remains off (no PWM).
100% Duty Cycle: If WIDTH = 1023 for a given LED, that LED turns on and remains on (no PWM).
The PHASE and WIDTH register addresses are from 0x00 to 0x6F.
8.5.1.1 Most Significant Bit-Packed PHASE/WIDTH Registers (MPHASE and MWIDTH)–Read/Write
The most significant bit-packed PHASE/WIDTH register map groups the most significant bits (MSBs) of the
PHASE and WIDTH values together. This makes a total of 16 PHASE registers (at addresses 00h through 0Fh)
and 16 WIDTH registers (at addresses 10h through 1Fh). It allows writing the least significant bits of
PHASE/WIDTH through a 3-byte write command for subtle light contrast without touching the major brightness
change per any LED sub-string.
8.5.1.2 Least Significant Bit-Packed PHASE/WIDTH Registers (LPHASE and LWIDTH) –Read/Write
The least significant bit-packed register map groups the two least significant bits (LSBs) of each LED PHASE
and WIDTH value together and places these packed registers at the end of each register set. This makes a total
of 16 PHASE registers (at addresses 20h through 2Fh) and 16 WIDTH registers (at addresses 30h through 3Fh).
It allows writing the most significant bits of PHASE/WIDTH through a 12-byte write command for major
brightness change without considering subtle light contrast for all LEDs.
8.5.1.3 Discrete LED PHASE/WIDTH Registers (DPHASE and DWIDTH) –Read/Write
The discrete LED PHASE/WIDTH register map allows all twenty bits (10 bits per PHASE and 10 bits per WIDTH)
to be written or read for a single LED in one four-byte write/read transaction without a need to write or read other
LED settings. This makes a total of 48 registers (at addresses 40h through 6Fh).

9.1 Application Information
The TPS92662A-Q1 device is capable of shunting any combination of 12-series LEDs at high frequency and at
variable duty cycles. The device includes four sets of 3×series-connected integrated switches for bypassing
individual LEDs. The individual sub-strings make the TPS92662A-Q1 device capable of handling single or
multiple current sources, as well as paralleling up to four switches for bypassing high current LEDs. Figure 33
illustrates a dynamic headlight application using multiple TPS92662A-Q1 devices on multiple boards. The
electronic control unit (ECU), usually attached to the outside of the headlight, contains the master MCU and a
boost pre-regulator stage that takes the variable battery voltage and steps it up to a stable DC voltage rail. The
application also includes multiple channels of buck current regulators to provide a stable current for each series
string of high brightness LEDs. For the best performance, the TPS92662A-Q1 device must reside on the LED
load board where it is as close as possible to the LEDs to which it directly connects.

9.1.1 Guidelines For Current Source
The TPS92662A-Q1 device is developed considering a high-side sensing hysteretic buck current source. This
type of high bandwidth current source is recommended to power the LED strings. The recommended guidelines
for the current source are as follows:
• Operate the current source switching converter in CCM (continuous conduction mode).
• It is recommended to operate at a switching frequency between 250 times the PWM frequency and 2000
times the PWM frequency.
• Minimize the capacitance at the output of the current source between the top of the string (sub-string) and the
GND to avoid excessive overshoot current through the un-shunted LEDs when PWM dimming is applied.
• Minimize capacitance across each LED to avoid excessive overshoot current through the TPS92662A-Q1
shunt switch when PWM dimming is applied.
• Follow the Device and Documentation Support .

10 Power Supply Recommendations
10.1 General Recommendations
The TPS92662A-Q1 device requires a supply voltage on the VIN pin (V IN range: 4.5 V to 60 V) to power the
charge pump, internal logic, and references. The VIN rail generates multiple low voltage rails of VDD = 5.0 V,
which is used for the internal analog blocks, digital communications, and the internal ADCs as outlined in the
Internal Regulators section. The TPS92662A-Q1 device is only compatible with 5-V logic levels.
10.2 Internal Regulators
Figure 41 showsz= the on-board LDOs of the TPS92662A-Q1 device and the associated pins. All the power pins
must be bypassed by a 0.1-µF ceramic capacitor.
Figure 41. TPS92662A-Q1 Internal Regulator
If the V IN supply is equivalent or less than 5 V, the VIN pin must be connected to the VDD pin. This connection
bypasses the internal VDD LDO as shown in Figure 42.
Figure 42. Power Pin Connections for VIN ≤ 5 V
The VDD pin is the positive voltage rail for the digital I/Os of the device.
10.3 Power Up and Reset
VIN must be greater than VIN (UVLO-R) prior to sourcing current through the LED string to ensure a controlled start-
up. See the Failsafe Mode At Power-Up section for more details on the initial condition of the bypass switches
upon power-up.
10.4 V IN Power Consumption
Power consumption increases with increased clock frequency, VIN supply, and temperature. It is always best to
select lower V IN and the lowest clock frequency that the system can tolerate.

11 Layout
11.1 Layout Guidelines
The system-level configuration of an LED matrix application varies greatly. Layout considerations for passive
components follow a simple rule: place the VDD and VIN decoupling capacitors close to the device and make the
traces between the CPPx pins and the associated bypass capacitors short. The challenge is to develop a careful
layout plan that efficiently routes the current source to the LED strings. The communication pins are designed for
ease of routing on a single-sided, metal core board. Each communications pin has a duplicate pin on the
opposite side of the device, allowing multiple devices to use a daisy chain configuration.
